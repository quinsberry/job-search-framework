# Job Search Framework

The framework helps a job seeker assess vacancies and prepare compensation expectations for interviews.

## Language

**Candidate profile**:
The candidate's source-backed professional facts, including experience, skills, projects, education and languages. It is distinct from search preferences, compensation expectations and vacancy-specific application materials.
_Avoid_: tailored CV, search criteria

**Search criteria**:
The job seeker's requirements and preferences used to discover and assess vacancies, including role, compensation and location eligibility.

**Search configuration**:
The job seeker's editable choices for a vacancy search, linked to their professional profile. It can contain unfinished decisions; past experience alone does not establish desired roles or mandatory vacancy requirements.

**Qualifying vacancy**:
A vacancy with evidence satisfying every mandatory search criterion and no confirmed exclusion. Qualifying vacancies count toward the requested vacancy count.
_Avoid_: inspected listing, possible match

**Vacancy needing clarification**:
A vacancy with missing or ambiguous evidence for at least one mandatory search criterion and no confirmed disqualifying condition.
_Avoid_: qualifying vacancy, confirmed match

**Advertised compensation**:
The employer's published fixed-cash pay amount or range for a vacancy, with its contract type, currency, pay period and pay basis. It is distinct from the candidate's asking range and an employer offer.

**Discovery budget**:
The limit on search effort used to find qualifying vacancies. It is separate from the requested count of qualifying vacancies.

**Vacancy snapshot**:
The vacancy facts and supporting evidence observed at a recorded time. It preserves what was known for a search assessment even when the live posting later changes.

**Duplicate vacancy**:
Another appearance of the same hiring opening, including appearances in different sources or search runs. Similar company names and titles alone do not establish that two listings describe the same opening.

**Vacancy history**:
The recorded observations of a hiring opening across searches. It distinguishes changes to the opening from the progress or outcome of an individual candidacy.

**Tracked vacancy**:
A hiring opening retained for later review, including inspected listings and openings brought in from an existing application. Being tracked does not establish fit or submission.

**Submission mark**:
The candidate's explicit indication that an application was submitted. Correcting this mark to not applied is distinct from withdrawing an application that was submitted. A submission can have an unknown date.

**Not observed vacancy**:
A previously known vacancy that does not appear in a particular later search. This does not establish that the vacancy is closed or that a candidacy was rejected.

**Vacancy review choice**:
The candidate's explicit decision to leave a saved vacancy unreviewed, shortlist it, or dismiss it. It is separate from evidence-based fit and application status, and survives posting changes.

**Hiring task**:
An action the candidate needs to take during the hiring process, such as preparing for an interview, following up with a recruiter, or deciding on an offer. It is distinct from an interview stage or application status.

**Asking range**:
A defensible fixed-cash compensation range the job seeker can state to a recruiter for a specific vacancy. It reflects comparable pay in the job seeker's residence market and the vacancy's circumstances; bonus and equity are separate.
_Avoid_: precise salary, guaranteed offer

**Application salary**:
The candidate's fixed-cash asking range for an application, with its currency, pay period, contract type, and pay basis. It may be explicitly unknown; advertised vacancy pay and employer offers are separate information.
_Avoid_: advertised salary, offered salary

**Residence market**:
The job seeker's location used to select local compensation comparisons, including for a remote vacancy.
_Avoid_: employer headquarters market

**Role scope**:
The duties, responsibilities, required skills, and required knowledge of a vacancy that determine the level of work expected. The vacancy title is only a label and may not reflect this scope.
_Avoid_: role title

**Vacancy type**:
The work arrangement for a specific vacancy: Product means building the hiring company's own product; Outsource means delivering a client project through a provider; Outstaff means working within a client team while supplied by a provider. A company can have vacancies of different types.
_Avoid_: company model, contract type

**Contract type**:
The legal and commercial arrangement under which the job seeker would be paid, such as employment or contractor/B2B. It is distinct from the vacancy type.
_Avoid_: vacancy type

**Work location arrangement**:
Whether a vacancy requires remote, hybrid, or onsite work. It is distinct from the vacancy type and contract type.
_Avoid_: vacancy type, contract type

**Application record**:
A tracked candidacy for a specific vacancy, including preparation before submission and subsequent hiring progress.
_Avoid_: submitted application when referring to a record that is still ready

**Application status**:
The overall state or outcome of an application record: ready, applied, screening, interviewing, offer, rejected, withdraw, or closed. Interviewing covers the interview process; individual interviews are stages rather than application statuses.

**Application outcome**:
The disposition of a candidacy: rejected means the employer declined it, withdraw means the candidate withdrew, and closed means the vacancy ended without an individual rejection.

**Application notes**:
Candidate-provided context about an application, including references to where the latest recruiter conversation can be found.

**Behavioral interview**:
An interview focused on past behavior, collaboration, judgment, and interpersonal skills.

**Leadership interview**:
An interview focused on alignment with company leadership, expectations, and the candidacy, rather than a technical assessment. The interviewer's title alone does not determine the interview's purpose.

**Application ID**:
A stable identifier for one application record, distinguishing separate candidacies even when they share a vacancy link.

**Interview stages**:
The expected sequence of interview parts in a vacancy's hiring process, learned from the employer. The sequence may initially be unknown and is something the candidate should ask about during screening.

**Next interview**:
The upcoming interview part of a vacancy's hiring process, such as a second technical interview or a CEO interview, with a scheduled date and time when known.
_Avoid_: next action, next application task
